FDM print package for an under-desk, slide-in **laptop cradle**. Everything here
is generated by script — no manual CAD:

- Generator: `../desk_mounts.py` (FreeCAD, headless; all parameters at the top)
- Exports: `export/*.step` + `export/*.stl` (assembled coordinates, mm) and
  `export/manifest.json` (measurements)
- Preview renders: `export/render_<piece>.png`, `export/render_cradle_assembly.png`
  (iso / front / side), regenerated with `../stl_render.py`

Units mm. Desk underside = z 0; every piece hangs in −z. Rotate per the
orientation notes below for printing.

## What it is

Two mirrored **slide rails** carry the laptop at its left/right edges, plus a
two-piece **back stop**. The laptop (closed, powered off while docked) slides in
flat from the front:

- floor arms (30 mm wide) carry the laptop edges — the middle is open;
- **53 mm** between the desk underside and the floor top is the slide-in height
  (the reference laptop is 23.2 mm closed and a 15–30 mm item may ride on top; a
  laptop-only build is 32 mm — one parameter, `CRADLE_SLOT`);
- a 3 mm lip at the front edge of each arm stops it sliding back out; the back
  plate carries a **30 mm-deep support ledge** (top flush with the rail arms) so
  the laptop's rear edge rests on it — the plate itself is the travel stop;
- the back plate abuts the rail ends and stops rearward travel; the rails' side
  walls guide it;
- the L cross-section rail = floor arm + outboard wall + 18 mm top screw flange,
  with three 15 mm corner gussets.

Default slot is **360 mm** — parameter `CRADLE_INNER_W`. Measure the closed
laptop and adjust before printing; 360 fits up to ~16″, a 13–14″ machine only
needs ~320 (narrower = stiffer rail span and less desk coverage, all better).

Mounting: #6-class ~19 mm wood screws straight up through the top flanges into
the desk (4 per rail, 2 per back half = **12**), spread across 180 mm runs for a
thin desk. JB Weld under the flanges is the intended hybrid (epoxy carries
shear, screws clamp while curing).

## Print orientation

Rails — lay the **top flange on the bed** (part stands 35 mm tall); supports
under the floor arm underside only (that face points down when installed; the
**arm top is the laptop contact face — print it up**). Back halves — lay the
**flange underside on the bed** (the plate then stands as a 29 mm fin; the
30 mm ledge is a flat shelf and prints without support).

## Material + print settings

- **PA612-CF preferred, PETG-CF fallback** (PLA not recommended: the cradle
  lives in warm, loaded service; nylon absorbs moisture, so dry it; treat
  ribs/gussets as the load path, not the material).
- FDM, ~6 perimeters/walls, 20–30 % gyroid infill, 0.2–0.25 mm layers
  (perimeters carry the load, gyroid is the core).
- Layer-line strength: rails and back — layers run along the 180/199 mm length
  when printed in the orientations above.
- Estimated material: **~0.45–0.7 kg** of filament at those settings
  (solid volume ≈ 380 cm³ across the four pieces).

## Assembly

1. Dry-fit the two back halves (half-lap) and glue them; let set.
2. Place rails + back against the desk underside, dry, check the laptop slides
   freely (lip clears; slot clears).
3. Mark and drill pilot holes, epoxy the flanges, screw up. **Screws go through
   the flange from below into the desk.** JB Weld cures; screws stay.
4. Re-check: nothing protrudes above the desk; flange tops sit flush (they are
   the contact faces).

## Open items

- Exact laptop model / closed thickness — measure and set `CRADLE_INNER_W`;
  `CRADLE_SLOT` is the slide-in height.
- Screw length vs desk thickness — 19 mm suits ~18–20 mm desks; pick to avoid
  breakout.
- The reference laptop fit is a measured target, not a claim. Record the actual
  width/thickness and the printed fit.
